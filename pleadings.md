**Top-level (Main) Job**  
**As a defendant (or defendant’s stakeholders), resolve a formal legal claim that has been asserted against us in a way that protects our interests, minimizes overall harm (financial, operational, reputational, relational), and achieves the best available outcome under the circumstances.**

This is the stable, solution-agnostic progress the organization is trying to make once a pleading appears on the docket after investigation. It has a clear beginning (claim formally asserted), middle (assessment, response, engagement), and end (claim resolved or positioned for later resolution). It sits above any particular tactic (motion, answer, settlement, etc.).

### Mid-level Jobs (Major Sub-Objectives / Phases in the Idealized Workflow)
These are the primary chunks of progress that compose the top-level job. They form a logical sequence in an idealized job map (not a rigid court calendar).

1. **Understand and assess the formal claim and its implications**  
   Grasp what is being asserted, the stakes, the procedural posture, and the potential exposure so decisions can be grounded in reality.

2. **Formulate a coherent defensive strategy and response posture**  
   Decide the overall approach (challenge, contest, narrow, negotiate, or combination) that best advances the top-level job given the facts, law, risk tolerance, and business context.

3. **Preserve and organize relevant information and positions**  
   Ensure evidence, documents, knowledge, and internal alignment are secured and structured so the defense can be supported and later stages do not create new liabilities.

4. **Execute the initial formal response and establish the defensive position**  
   Place a timely, effective response on the record that protects rights, raises available defenses, and sets the stage for the desired trajectory of the matter.

5. **Engage the opposing side and the process toward resolution or further progress**  
   Open pathways (negotiation, motion practice, early case management) that move the claim toward a favorable or acceptable conclusion while managing cost and distraction.

6. **Monitor, adapt, and close out the early phase**  
   Track developments, adjust as new information appears, and transition cleanly into discovery, motion practice, or resolution once the initial response phase is complete.

### Micro Jobs (Granular Steps that Compose the Mid-Level Jobs)
These are the more atomic, chronological actions within an idealized process. They remain technology- and solution-agnostic (no assumption of particular software, specific court rules language, or even that outside counsel is used). Grouped under the mid-level jobs for clarity:

**Under “Understand and assess the formal claim…”**
- Identify and obtain the complete set of formal papers and any associated docket information.
- Parse the claims, factual allegations, legal theories, and requested relief.
- Map the asserted claims against known facts, prior investigation, contracts, policies, and potential defenses.
- Estimate range of exposure (damages, injunctive risk, collateral consequences, insurance implications).
- Identify jurisdictional, procedural, or pleading defects and any immediate deadlines or waiver risks.
- Clarify internal stakeholders, decision rights, and any parallel regulatory, insurance, or business constraints.

**Under “Formulate a coherent defensive strategy…”**
- Generate and compare realistic pathways (early challenge, full contest, narrowing, early resolution outreach, etc.).
- Weigh trade-offs of cost, time, precedent risk, relationship impact, and probability of different outcomes.
- Align internal decision-makers on risk tolerance, settlement authority parameters, and communication protocols.
- Select the initial response vehicle(s) and the broader case theory or narrative that will guide later stages.
- Identify any affirmative claims, third-party issues, or insurance tender opportunities that should be asserted early.

**Under “Preserve and organize relevant information…”**
- Issue and document litigation holds / preservation directives to relevant custodians.
- Locate, secure, and begin cataloging key documents, data, and potential witnesses.
- Capture and organize the pre-existing investigation file and any contemporaneous knowledge.
- Establish a working internal record of positions, admissions to avoid, and open questions.
- Assess privilege, work-product, and confidentiality boundaries early.

**Under “Execute the initial formal response…”**
- Draft or prepare the chosen response (admissions/denials, affirmative defenses, challenges, counter/cross claims as appropriate).
- Ensure the response is complete, timely, and procedurally compliant while preserving all viable positions.
- Coordinate any required supporting materials or notices.
- File/serve the response and confirm it is properly on the record.
- Communicate the response and its implications internally and (where appropriate) to insurers or other stakeholders.

**Under “Engage the opposing side and the process…”**
- Initiate or respond to early dialogue about resolution, narrowing, or case management if strategically useful.
- Prepare for and participate in any required early conferences or scheduling events.
- Raise or address any immediate procedural or protective issues.
- Begin positioning for the next phase (discovery parameters, motion practice, mediation windows, etc.).

**Under “Monitor, adapt, and close out the early phase…”**
- Track all deadlines, filings, and communications.
- Reassess strategy as the response lands and any early reactions appear.
- Document lessons, open items, and hand-off points for the subsequent phase of the matter.
- Confirm the claim is now properly joined and the defense is positioned for whatever comes next.

### Notes on Using This Structure (Kalbach-Style)
- Keep statements solution-agnostic: the jobs describe progress the defendant is trying to make, not “file a 12(b)(6) motion” or “use e-discovery software.”
- Circumstances matter: the relative weight of these jobs shifts with claim type, jurisdiction, relationship with the plaintiff, insurance status, public visibility, etc.
- Emotional/social layers sit on top of the functional core (e.g., reduce internal anxiety and uncertainty, protect reputation with customers/regulators/employees, demonstrate competent stewardship to the board).
- Desired outcomes can later be made measurable (minimize time-to-clarity on exposure, maximize preservation of viable defenses, minimize unnecessary cost in the response window, etc.).
- This is an idealized workflow. Real cases involve iteration, surprises, and parallel tracks; the job map helps surface where friction or opportunity typically appears.

This hierarchy gives a clean starting scaffold for a Kalbach-style job map and subsequent outcome statements or opportunity scoring focused on the post-pleading defendant posture. If you want to expand any mid-level job into a fuller step-by-step job map, add emotional/social jobs, or shift the altitude (e.g., narrower commercial contract case vs. broader class action), just say the word.
